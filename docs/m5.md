## Where it started

- Worldgen has one biome (meadow) in `data/worldgen/biomes.json`. `stage_biomes.gd` already
  splits the width into a strip by weight and width.
- Three vocabularies disagree. Worldgen says `meadow`; the sim (`data/sim/biomes.json`,
  `world_sim.gd`) says `plains`, `forest`, `hills`, `mountains`, `coast`, `desert`, `swamp`,
  `tundra`; `data/ecology/habitats.json` says `meadow`, `forest`, `hills`, `swamp`, `desert`,
  `arctic`, `coast`, `caves`, `underdark`.
- `world_sim.gd:91` has a biome strip of its own for its stand-alone layout. Check that
  sites on a generated world take the biome of the ground they stand on.
- Habitats already cover the set: forest 107 monsters, hills 89, meadow 76, swamp 40,
  desert 28. Mountains have no row of their own and take hills until they earn one.
- Generation takes 2.5 s at dev size and 8.6 s at small. Medium is unmeasured; my estimate is
  about 30 s, and `passages` is the stage to watch if it runs long.

## What this milestone delivers

1. **One biome vocabulary.** A single list of ids, used by worldgen, the sim, habitats and
   places, with a test that fails if any table names an unknown id.
2. **A climate journey.** The width follows a temperature curve, so neighbours make sense and
   the two ends differ. Worlds vary in the widths and in which of two orders they take.
   Each surface biome has its own height profile, top soil, stone and walls.
3. **Natural barriers.** Mountain ranges and rivers separate regions. Each has a way across:
   passes, bridges or fords, roads that use them, and cave routes under the range. Barriers
   slow travel without sealing it, and the passability numbers must hold.
   Built: [tracks/barriers.md](tracks/barriers.md).
4. **Biomes in the sim.** A site takes the biome under it. Biomes set food and goods
   capacity, so farms feed meadows, forests sell timber, mountains sell ore and stone, deserts
   trade scarce water and salt, and swamps sell reagents. Cultures settle where they fit.
   Long routes between unlike regions become the valuable ones, and worth raiding.
   Built: [tracks/sim-biomes.md](tracks/sim-biomes.md).
5. **Biomes you can see.** Tile palettes, sky and light tint, and dressing per biome.
   Settlement styles follow culture and climate. Synty nature packs supply the props.
6. **Monsters that belong.** The spawn director and lairs read the real biome through
   `habitats.json`, so a desert feels unlike a swamp in what hunts you.
7. **Place names on the map.** Regions get names, and the world map shows regions, ranges
   and rivers.

## How it gets built

Contracts first (end of `docs/architecture.md`), then Opus agents in worktrees, each reporting
in `docs/tracks/`. While ComfyUI is busy: one agent and one Godot process at a time.

- **Wave 1**
  - Vocabulary and climate: the shared ids, temperature curve, height profiles, palettes.
  - Barriers: ranges, rivers, crossings, roads and passages through them.
- **Wave 2**
  - Sim: site biomes, capacities, goods and culture fit, then re-tuning the sweep.
  - Look and life: sky, dressing, place styles, spawning, region names, the map.
- **Then** an integration pass, and you play it.

## Verification

- The suite and every smoke variant stay green, and the end-to-end test stays unseeded.
- `tools/worldmap.gd` pictures of several seeds, which I look at: the order reads as a
  journey, and no biome is a sliver.
- Passability holds on medium: point-to-point at 85% or better, every site reachable, every
  barrier crossed on foot without digging.
- Medium generation time is measured and reported. About 30 s is fine (user, 2026-11-06).
- A sim sweep across seeds: scarcity moves goods, long routes carry more value, no biome is
  always starving or always rich, and worlds stay healthy over decades.
- A walk across a whole world in pictures, one per biome, by day and night.
- You play it.

## Open questions
