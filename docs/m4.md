# Godspark M4: the living world on screen

## Direction (user, 2026-11-05, after playing M2)

The game steps away from Terraria. Building and collecting materials are not the point.
The point is a world that changes, the simulation behind it, and adventuring through the
overworld, the Underdark, the Hells and different biomes, all connected naturally. The
player is still allowed to dig.

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Realms | One continuous descent: surface, caves, Underdark, then the deepest rifts open into the Hells below, with no loading break. Other planes are reached through crossings at fitting places that open and close with what happens in the world |
| Digging | Dig anything diggable; it gives plain rubble you can place again to bridge or block. No ores to farm, no block types to collect. Camp gear is bought, found, or made from one supply item |
| Gear | From loot, from living smiths whose stock and quality come from the sim, and from quests and patrons |
| Passable world | Areas must be passable without digging. Ropes you anchor and climb (W up, S down). Stairs and pathways that crisscross; S drops through a walkway when there is open space below |
| Next | The living world on screen |

What stops growing: crafting, ore tiers, block variety. Materials stay a property of gear;
ore is a trade good the sim moves and something you can sell.

## What this milestone delivers

1. **A world you can walk.** Every cave region, cavern, site and road is reachable by a
   Medium walker without digging: stairs, walkways at several heights that cross each
   other, ladders and hanging ropes, natural ramps. Monsters use the same ways.
2. **Places.** Settlements are real: houses, a smithy, a market, a tavern, a shrine, walls
   or a gate for bigger ones, built from tile stamps with Synty pieces as the skin (shown as
   a cutaway), in the culture's style. They grow when the sim says the settlement grew and
   fall into ruin when it is abandoned. Roads join them on the surface; deep roads below.
3. **People and parties.** When a player comes near, the sim's settlement shows its
   people (townsfolk on a daily round, guards from the real garrison), caravans travel the
   roads as wagons with escorts, raiders come up from the deep roads, patrols walk their
   routes. A fight you watch is fought in real time; its outcome is written back to the sim.
   One you do not watch is resolved by the sim as before.
4. **Dealings** (second wave). Talk to people: rumours are real events with places.
   Shops and smiths sell what the settlement actually has, at its prices. Quests and
   patrons come from the sim's state (escort this caravan, clear the lair starving that
   town, win back stolen cargo) and pay in coin, gear and standing. A map and a quest log.
5. **Standing.** Each faction remembers what you did. It moves prices, who attacks you,
   and what patrons offer. Bonds from Knacks feed into it.

## How it gets built

Contracts first (end of `docs/architecture.md`), then Opus agents in worktrees, three at a
time, each reporting in `docs/tracks/`.

- **Wave 1**
  - Traversal and rubble: S to drop, ropes, stairs and walkways, a worldgen pass that
    guarantees passable caves and ways down, rubble-only digging, the supply item.
  - Places: settlement stamps and skins per culture, roads, growth and ruin, the
    `Places` data other tracks read.
  - Life: materialization (appear, freeze, fold back), townsfolk and guard brains,
    caravans, raids and patrols on route paths, faction stance in targeting, write-back.
- **Wave 2**
  - Dealings: talk, shops and smiths from sim stock, quests and patrons, standing.
  - Interface: dialogue, shop, quest log, world map, place names on arrival.
- **Then** an integration pass, and you play it.

Later milestones, in the new order: biomes and the shape of the overworld; the Underdark
as places and peoples; the continuous descent into the Hells; crossings to other planes;
apotheosis.
